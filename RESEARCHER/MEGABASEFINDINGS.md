# MEGABASEFINDINGS.md — Ted & Eric Ruud, across the archive

Read-only sweep of `C:\Dev\megabase\megabase.db` (1.45M messages, 5,271
conversations, 11 sources) for every real trace of Eric Ruud, cross-referenced
against `SYNTHBUILDER/research/tideroom_lessons.md` (the only prior work on
Eric) and Ted's own interest-cluster docs. Method: exact-string search for
"Eric Ruud", "EricRuud", "tide-room", "tideroom", plus a filtered pass over
bare "Eric" (31,378 raw hits — almost entirely noise, see below); every
conversation with a real, non-trivial hit was pulled in full and read.

## Honest headline finding

**tide-room itself never appears in the archive.** Zero hits for "tide-room"
or "tideroom" anywhere across all 11 sources. Ted's only contact with the
project is the external GitHub read already captured in
`research/tideroom_lessons.md` — there is no chat message, email, or text
where Ted and Eric discuss the repo, JUCE, DSP validation, or spatial synths.
Whatever connects Ted's and Eric's *technical* practice has to be inferred
from older, adjacent evidence (both made music; both did things in games), not
from any conversation about tide-room's actual content.

What **does** exist is a long, dense personal friendship — 700+ conversations
mentioning "Eric Ruud" across SMS, Facebook, and Twitter, a 16-year friendship
as of 2020 (Facebook's own "16 years of friendship" notice), and, unexpectedly,
a real shared music-making history from college that predates tide-room by
about two decades. That's the actual find here: not a technical correspondence,
but enough biographical depth to characterize Eric as more than "friend who
sent a repo link."

## Genuine points of connection

1. **Both make/made music seriously, including together.** This is the
   strongest, most concrete link, and it predates tide-room by ~20 years:
   college-era joint recordings, Eric's band with a Spotify release, Eric's
   own dedicated rehearsal space. Ted's chiptune/NES-fidelity work and Eric's
   band recordings are different genres, but "cares enough about audio
   production to argue about what makes a mix sound smooth" is shared ground.
2. **A verify-the-real-thing instinct, weakly attested on Eric's side.** The
   "smooth mixes are deceptively hard" comment and the envelope-modulation
   joke are thin but real evidence Eric thinks about audio production as a
   craft with a right answer you have to actually hear — the same instinct
   `tideroom_lessons.md` documents structurally in `criteria.json` and
   `evaluate.py`'s "load the actual built VST3 in a real plugin host" gate.
   This is a plausible, not proven, throughline; the corpus doesn't contain
   enough of Eric's own words about process to call it confirmed.
3. **Overlapping "systems that generate meaning" sensibility.** Eric argues
   real positions on textual interpretation, tradition, and anachronism in
   the Finnegans Wake / "western esotericism" thread — adjacent to, though
   not the same as, Ted's game-design-from-historical-esoteric-systems work
   (EmblemRoguelike, SigilForge, KabbalahTrainer, etc.).
4. **Games as a shared context.** Eric's games-industry job, GDC attendance,
   and MTG cube-draft vocabulary overlap with Ted's own game-design practice
   (MTGSLIDER, the roguelike cluster, DungeonAB) even though neither side of
   the correspondence ever connects this to synthesis work specifically.
5. **Early, independent interest in generative/computational tools on both
   sides** (Eric's 2016 DeepForger dabbling; Ted's much later, much heavier
   LLM-driven build practice) — coincidental parallel more than a documented
   collaboration.

## Genuine points of divergence

1. **Domain and toolchain don't overlap at all in evidence.** Eric's
   documented music-making (Legubitron, bedroom lo-fi, "Brandon" tracks) is
   conventional band/DAW recording, not DSP/plugin engineering — tide-room's
   JUCE/Metal/ML-identification apparatus is not foreshadowed anywhere in the
   personal correspondence. It may be a newer development in Eric's life that
   simply postdates the years the archive covers most densely (bulk of the
   rich material is 2014–2021; SMS thread runs to 2026 but goes quiet on
   technical topics).
2. **Ted's esoteric/occult material is Ted-originated in every thread found.**
   There's no evidence Eric independently studies Kabbalah, alchemy, or
   tarot; he engages with Ted's framing (competently, sometimes at length)
   rather than bringing parallel material of his own.
3. **No shared technical vocabulary around NES/chiptune fidelity, JSFX,
   REAPER, or the verification apparatus SYNTHBUILDER actually cares about.**
   Nothing in the archive suggests Eric knows or has opinions on APU
   emulation, duty-cycle synthesis, or REAPER's RPP format — SYNTHBUILDER's
   entire subject matter is outside anything Eric and Ted are shown
   discussing.
4. **tide-room's actual engineering discipline is undocumented in
   correspondence.** Everything `tideroom_lessons.md` knows about Eric's
   validation rigor comes from reading the repo, not from Eric describing his
   own process to Ted. The "genuine connection" in point 2 above is an
   inference bridging two different eras of evidence (2014–2021 personal
   correspondence vs. a 2020s-era GitHub repo), not a documented throughline.

## Bottom line

The corpus confirms a real, long, warm friendship with a surprising amount of
shared music-making history — enough to make "Eric shared his synth plugin
repo" read as continuity of an old collaboration rather than a cold link. But
it does not contain any direct discussion of tide-room, DSP validation,
JUCE, or plugin engineering, and Ted's esoteric/occult interests appear to be
his own rather than mutually cultivated. The strongest honest connection is
biographical (two people who have made music together and separately for two
decades) rather than technical; the technical parallel drawn in
`tideroom_lessons.md` (validate against the real running artifact) is
plausible given Eric's one stray comment about mix difficulty, but should be
labeled inference, not confirmed shared methodology.
