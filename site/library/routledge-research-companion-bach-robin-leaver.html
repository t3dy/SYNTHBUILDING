The Routledge Research Companion to Johann Sebastian Bach

Robin A. Leaver (ed.) · E:\pdf\music\Bach\Robin A. Leaver (ed.) - The Routledge research companion to Johann Sebastian Bach (2013, Ashgate) - libgen.li.pdf

Edited by Robin A. Leaver, this Routledge/Ashgate volume (2013) is explicitly pitched at 'the next level' of Bach research — masters and doctoral students and established scholars — rather than as a general reference or introductory companion (the front matter distinguishes it directly from lexicon-style works like the Oxford Composer Companion: J.S. Bach). Each contributor is assigned three tasks within a specific subject area: (1) summarize the significant and controversial research of the past thirty to fifty years — examples cited include the debate over Friedrich Smend's Neue Bach-Ausgabe edition of the B minor Mass, Friedrich Blume's conclusions about Bach's religious commitment following the 'new chronology' of the cantatas (established via paper and handwriting studies), Joshua Rifkin's controversial one-voice-per-part reconstruction of Bach's vocal forces, and the rediscovery of the Berlin Sing-Akademie manuscript collection in Kiev after the Cold War; (2) critically analyze current research opening new methodological ground, such as Martin Jarvis's contested forensic-handwriting claim that Anna Magdalena Bach composed the cello suites, and ongoing work tracing Bach's use of the Lutheran chorale tradition; and (3) identify areas still needing investigation. The result is a state-of-the-field survey organized by topic (chronology and sources, theology and text, instrumental and vocal genres, reception and performance practice) rather than a biography or a listener's guide, deliberately written to orient a researcher entering Bach studies toward the live scholarly controversies rather than settled facts. For SYNTHBUILDER this is not a technical resource at all — no synthesis, DSP, or sound-programming content — but as general music-scholarship background it is the single best-organized map in the library of what current Bach research actually argues about (chronology, authorship, performance practice), which is useful context if Ted's other Bach-adjacent projects (e.g. CHIPPEDONBACH, referenced in SYNTHBUILDER's own docs) ever need to justify a source-critical choice about which version of a Bach piece to arrange or chiptune.

Relevance to SYNTHBUILDER
Low — a graduate-level state-of-the-field survey of Bach musicology and source criticism, with no synthesis/DSP content; background only for any Bach-arrangement work.
