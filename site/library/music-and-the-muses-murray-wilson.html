Music and the Muses: The Culture of Mousike in the Classical Athenian City (Oxford University Press, 2004), edited by Penelope Murray and Peter Wilson, is a scholarly essay collection on mousike — the ancient Greek concept combining music, poetry, and dance into a single cultural practice, rather than treating 'music' as the separable art form modern usage implies — and its central role in the civic, religious, and educational life of classical Athens. The book's chapters, contributed by specialists in Greek literature, history, and musicology, likely address topics such as choral performance at civic festivals (the City Dionysia, other Athenian festivals), music's role in Athenian education (mousike as one of the two core disciplines alongside gymnastike in the traditional Greek educational scheme), the professionalization of musicians and debates about that professionalization in Greek sources, and the relationship between musical practice and Athenian democratic/civic identity.
