Johann Sebastian Bach's Art of Fugue: Performance Practice Based on German Eighteenth-Century Theory

Ewald Demeyere · E:\pdf\music\Bach\Ewald Demeyere - Johann Sebastian Bach’s Art of Fugue _ Performance Practice Based on German Eighteenth-Century Theory (2017, Leuven University Press) - libgen.li.pdf

This is a musicological monograph (Leuven University Press, 2017) arguing that the performance of Bach's Art of Fugue (BWV 1080) — a set of fourteen fugues and four canons on a single subject, left without specified instrumentation and unfinished at Bach's death — has been distorted by two centuries of Romantic and modernist assumptions about what 'abstract' or 'purely intellectual' contrapuntal music should sound like. Demeyere's method is to reconstruct period German performance practice from eighteenth-century treatises (Quantz, C.P.E. Bach, Türk, Kirnberger, and others in Bach's own circle) and apply those norms — tempo relationships between movements, articulation of subject entries, appropriate ornamentation, notes inégales-adjacent rhythmic inflection, and the vexed question of whether the work was conceived for a single keyboard, two keyboards, or an unspecified consort — directly to the Art of Fugue's individual movements. Chapters proceed roughly through the theoretical framework (rhetoric, affect, and fugal theory in the German tradition) and then through practice-oriented analysis of specific contrapuncti, including the famously unfinished final quadruple fugue (Contrapunctus XIV) and the disputed status of the 'mirror fugues.' The book is written for advanced keyboardists, harpsichordists/organists, conductors, and musicologists already fluent in species counterpoint and historical performance practice (HIP) debates — it assumes score-reading fluency and familiarity with fugal terminology (subject, answer, countersubject, stretto, augmentation, inversion) throughout, and is not an introduction to Bach or to fugue as a form. For SYNTHBUILDER specifically, this book is essentially irrelevant to synthesis, DSP, or plugin building — it is a historical-performance-practice study of a specific keyboard repertoire, not music theory in the compositional-craft sense and not remotely about sound technology. Its value to Ted's broader practice is as deep Bach/counterpoint background (useful if a future SYNTHBUILDER or FUGUEJUKEBOX-style project wants to generate or arrange fugal material with historically informed phrasing) rather than as a synthesis or chiptune-programming resource. It belongs on the showcase page as music-history/Bach-scholarship depth, not as a technical manual.
