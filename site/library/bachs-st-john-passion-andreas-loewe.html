Johann Sebastian Bach's St John Passion (BWV 245): A Theological Commentary

Andreas Loewe · E:\pdf\music\Bach\Johann Sebastian Bach’s St John Passion (BWV 245)_ A Theological Commentary __ {Loewe, Andreas}(2014 April 03, BRILL)[10.1163_9789004272361]{55664691} libgen.li.pdf

This is a scholarly theological-musicological commentary on one of Bach's two surviving Passion settings, the St John Passion (BWV 245, first performed Good Friday 1724 in Leipzig). Loewe's central argument is that the Passion cannot be fully understood as 'just' a musical masterpiece: its text (assembled from the Gospel of John, chorale verses, and newly written poetic meditations, likely with Bach's own theological input) is a carefully constructed piece of Lutheran devotional theology, and the music's rhetorical and affective choices — where a chorale interrupts the narrative, how the turba (crowd) choruses are harmonized, which arias comment on which narrative moments — are themselves theological arguments. The book proceeds through the work's structure, contextualizing Bach's Leipzig cantorate, the liturgical role of the Good Friday Vespers service the Passion was written for, the textual sources and revisions Bach made across several performing versions, and then close readings of individual movements that connect musical gesture (key choice, instrumentation, motivic word-painting) to specific points of Lutheran Christology and soteriology. It is written for musicologists, theologians, and advanced choral conductors/performers who already know the piece and want its doctrinal substructure explained, not as a general-audience introduction to the Passion or to Bach. For SYNTHBUILDER this book has no direct technical relevance — it is theology and text-music analysis, not synthesis, DSP, or sound design, and its subject (a large-scale choral/orchestral sacred work) is about as far from NES-chip-accurate chiptune programming as the library gets. Its place on the showcase page is purely as evidence of the depth of Ted's Bach collection alongside the more practically synth-relevant titles, useful background if a future project ever wanted to arrange or chiptune-ify Bach vocal material with attention to its original structure and meaning.
