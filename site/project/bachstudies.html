Bach Studies (scholarship, not music)

STUB  C:\Dev\BachStudies · SQLite, Python (PyMuPDF), vanilla static HTML, GitHub Actions (unused)

BachStudies follows the same DH-framework pattern as several of Ted's scholarly portals: a corpus/ of 13 Bach-scholarship books (Wolff, Stauffer, Leaver, Demeyere's Art of Fugue, Rathey, Forkel, and others) converted from PDF to Markdown, feeding a SQLite database and a static-HTML build. Its CLAUDE.md states clean system invariants (data flows one direction, source to DB to HTML; the DB is the only source of truth) and its own roadmap marks several phases 'READY,' but on inspection those phases ran only shallowly.

The database currently holds 12 primary entities, 12 scholars, 12 bibliography entries, 12 dictionary terms (Fugue, Cantata, Chorale, Counterpoint, Basso continuo, Passion, Thomaskirche, Leipzig, Cothen, Weimar, Bach Revival, Baroque -- all genuinely reviewed and high-confidence), and zero essays. But the committed site/dictionary/ folder contains 13,815 HTML pages, of which all but those 12 are essentially empty stubs left over from an indiscriminate corpus-extraction pass whose output no longer matches the reseeded 12-row database -- a stale, unpruned build sitting in git.

BACHPEDAGOGY.md proposes an ambitious four-tier music-theory curriculum with short public-domain-MIDI audio snippets as illustrative clips -- the only place audio is mentioned anywhere in this project -- but it is entirely unbuilt: no theory_lessons table, no MIDI rendering code, nothing beyond the planning document. The repo has no git remote configured at all, so its GitHub Actions Pages-deploy workflow, though well-formed, has never actually run.

This is a text and research project with no music-composition or audio-production component. It should not be filed alongside the actual music-hacking projects in this catalog, despite sharing Bach's name with Chipped On Bach.

The project's own `docs/ROADMAP.md` marks its eight phases with real, differentiated statuses rather than a uniform "in progress": Phase 0 (Source Conversion), Phase 1 (Database Scaffold), Phase 2 (Corpus Extraction), Phase 4 (Site Generation), Phase 5 (Validation), and Phase 6 (Deployment) are all marked READY, while Phase 3 (AI Enrichment) and Phase 7 (Content Review) are marked PLANNED rather than started -- meaning the honest gap is specifically in the two phases that would have turned raw extracted text into reviewed, trustworthy essay content, exactly the gap that produced the 13,815 largely-empty dictionary stub pages described above.

The 13-book corpus is a real, confirmed list rather than a round number: it includes Demeyere's Art of Fugue (also separately cataloged in this library), Stauffer's Organ Preludes (also separately cataloged), Rathey's Bach's Major Vocal Works, Christoph Wolff's Johann Sebastian Bach: The Learned Musician (Norton, 2000), Leaver's edited Routledge Research Companion to J.S. Bach (also separately cataloged), Forkel's foundational early biography J.S. Bach: His Life, Art, and Work, Loewe's St John Passion theological commentary (also separately cataloged), the Pincus guitar-tablature volume (also separately cataloged), a Peters/Sanders collection of contextual Bach studies, a Delphi Masterworks edition and a second Delphi Great Composers volume on Bach, a beginner-oriented Dutkanicz volume titled My First Book of Bach, and a standalone Dover edition pairing the Art of the Fugue with the Musical Offering -- meaning four of the thirteen books feeding this text-only scholarship project are the exact same physical books already cataloged separately, with their own page_summary entries, in this music-technology library, a genuine and useful cross-reference between the two catalogs rather than a coincidence. `BACHPEDAGOGY.md`'s proposed four-tier curriculum is fully specified rather than a vague sketch: Tier 1 "Gateway" assumes no prior music knowledge and covers ten foundational topics (fugue, counterpoint, chorale, key, aria, canon, passion, oratorio, basso continuo, prelude); Tier 2 "Explorer" assumes basic music literacy and covers ten more topics including subject/answer relationships, invertible counterpoint, da capo and ritornello form, chorale harmonization, and text-painting; Tier 3 "Student" assumes the reader can already analyze chord progressions and covers ten topics including stretto, augmentation and diminution, permutation fugue, musical rhetoric, the Neapolitan sixth, and the galant-style controversy; and Tier 4 "Scholar" is explicitly graduate-level and research-oriented, covering eight topics including computational fugue analysis, Kirkendale's Quintilian-rhetoric hypothesis about fugal structure, the Rifkin one-voice-per-part performance-practice debate, source criticism and dating methods, and the stile antico tradition -- each tier's topic list cites a specific source within the 13-book corpus (for example, a given topic citing "Demeyere Ch. 1" or "Rathey Appendix B"), and the document's own appendix independently confirms the corpus size, stating its topics were "extracted from 13 corpus files (11M of scholarly text)." None of this curriculum has been built into working code -- no lesson-content database table, no audio-snippet rendering pipeline -- and it remains, honestly, a well-specified plan rather than a feature.
