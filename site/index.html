GlitchMario

STABLE C:\Dev\GlitchMario

A static documentary site narrating five days of trying to extract Super Mario Bros.' overworld theme from raw NES hardware registers, told as a timeline of 100 events, 17 'blunders,' and 30 NES-history milestones -- self-deprecating and specific, down to phase-inverting drums and a JSFX operator meaning exponentiation instead of XOR. NESMusicStudio

ACTIVE C:\Dev\NESMusicStudio

NSF/ROM -> MIDI -> REAPER/WAV/MP4 -> YouTube pipeline, possibly the actual current front-runner of the whole NES-music-tools cluster. Castlevania 1 complete (15/15 tracks, 0 pitch mismatches); Contra in progress (11/11 tracks, 96.6% volume match). Git-committed and actively worked, live site deployed.

NESjamtools

STABLE C:\Dev\NESjamtools

A REAPER-based NES virtual instrument, not just an asset library: MIDI scores (or a live keyboard) become NES-accurate audio via a custom multitimbral JSFX synth plus a separate high-fidelity Python renderer. Ships 50 games as ready REAPER projects, hybrid stem+MIDI projects for 4 games. Sibling 'Instrument' layer to NSFRIPPER's 'Archive'.

NSFRIPPER (NES Music Extraction Studio)

ACTIVE C:\Dev\NSFRIPPER

Reverse-engineers NES game music into MIDI and REAPER projects via 6502/NSF emulation, ROM-trace parsing against real hardware, and a custom JSFX synth. 321 games, 8,900+ MIDIs extracted, deployed as a browsable site. Includes a Bach-mashup side project. Active but stalled since April 2026 on an unmade listening decision.

REAPERBEYONDNES

ACTIVE C:\Dev\REAPERBEYONDNES

Declared successor extending NSFRIPPER's pipeline past the NES to GBA, SMS, Saturn, Genesis, SNES, GBC, and Virtual Boy. Heavily structured (per-chip project folders, its own agent-protocol document). Real, substantial work -- and not a git repository, a genuine loss risk.

ReapNES Studio (ReapNESClod)

ARCHIVED C:\Dev\ReapNES-Studio

REAPER-based NES 2A03 synth (JSFX + JUCE/VST3) with a 54K-preset corpus extracted from NES-MDB, project-generator scripts, and an analog console UI. Solved its core MIDI-keyboard-routing and DC-offset bugs, then was folded into NSFRIPPER on 2026-03-31 as the canonical synth source; no independent activity since.

Music book library

'The Temple of Music' by Robert Fludd

Peter Hauge (critical edition and commentary)

A modern critical edition (Ashgate, series 'Music Theory in Britain 1500-1700') of Robert Fludd's 1617-18 treatise Templum Musicae, edited and translated with commentary by Peter Hauge, presenting the Renaissance polymath's cosmological theory of music alongside period musical practice.

Ancient Greek Music

M. L. West

M. L. West's classicist standard reference reconstructs ancient Greek musical theory, notation, instruments and surviving fragments from scattered literary, epigraphic and papyrus evidence — the foundational modern study of the subject.

Babes in Toyland's Fontanelle (33 1/3 series)

Selena Chambers

A 33 1/3 series study of Babes in Toyland's 1992 major-label breakthrough Fontanelle, examining the Minneapolis trio's abrasive riot-grrrl-adjacent sound, gender politics, and place in early-1990s alternative/grunge history.

Beastie Boys' Paul's Boutique (33 1/3 series)

Dan LeRoy

A 33 1/3 series study of the Beastie Boys' 1989 Paul's Boutique, one of hip-hop's most densely sampled records, examining the Dust Brothers' layered sampling production and the album's initial commercial failure followed by long-term critical canonization.

Björk's Homogenic (33 1/3 series)

Emily Mackay

A 33 1/3 series entry: Emily Mackay's critical/biographical study of Björk's 1997 album Homogenic, examining its blend of Icelandic strings, glitch-inflected beats and volcanic-landscape imagery as a landmark in electronic-organic pop production.

Companion to Medieval and Renaissance Music

Tess Knighton and David Fallows (eds.)

A 1992 Schirmer Books essay collection surveying medieval and Renaissance music (c. 500-1600), opening with essays interrogating modern value judgments about early music before moving through genre-, region-, and composer-focused specialist chapters.

Dark Waves: The Synthesizer and the Dystopian Sound of Britain (1977-80)

Neil O'Connor

A Rowman & Littlefield cultural-history monograph (part of the 'Popular Musics Matter' series) tracing how British synth-pop and post-punk acts of 1977-80 used the synthesizer's alien, 'inhuman' sonic character to voice Cold War and post-industrial dystopian anxiety.

Designing Software Synthesizer Plug-Ins in C++: For RackAFX, VST3, and Audio Units

Will Pirkle

Will Pirkle's standard textbook (Focal Press/Routledge) for building real synthesizer plugins in C++, covering oscillators, envelope generators, filters (including a full Moog ladder-filter implementation), LFOs, and complete synth-voice architecture across RackAFX, VST3 and Audio Units.

Devo's Freedom of Choice (33 1/3 series)

Evie Nagy

A 33 1/3 series study of Devo's 1980 breakthrough Freedom of Choice (home of 'Whip It'), examining the band's synth-pop crossover, satirical de-evolution concept, and use of early synthesizer technology to achieve mainstream new-wave success.

Four Ways of Hearing Video Game Music

Michiel Kamp

An Oxford University Press ludomusicology monograph proposing four distinct listening modes for engaging with video game music, building theoretical frameworks for how players actually perceive and process game scores during interactive play.

Game Sound: An Introduction to the History, Theory, and Practice of Video Game Music and Sound Design

Karen Collins

Karen Collins's MIT Press textbook is the foundational academic survey of video game audio, tracing the technical history from early arcade/console sound chips (including the NES) through MIDI-era and modern interactive scoring, alongside theoretical frameworks for game-sound analysis.

Hole's Live Through This (33 1/3 series)

Anwen Crawford

A 33 1/3 series study of Hole's 1994 album Live Through This, opening with a dedication and a Euripides epigraph on reputation, examining Courtney Love's songwriting, the album's fraught release context, and its place in 1990s alternative-rock and feminist music history.

How To Make A Noise: A Comprehensive Guide to Synthesizer Programming

Simon Cann

Simon Cann's hands-on synth-programming manual teaches subtractive, FM, wave-sequencing, and additive synthesis by building real patches (bass, pads, leads, drums) on six software synths, walking through oscillators, envelopes, filters, modulation, and FX one concept at a time.

J. S. Bach in Tablature, Volume 1: Beginning to Intermediate Pieces in Standard Tab and Notation for Guitar

Johann Sebastian Bach / arr. Jake Pincus

A Mel Bay 'Editiones Classicae' guitar-tablature songbook: beginning-to-intermediate Bach pieces arranged for classical guitar in both standard notation and tab, arranged/edited by Jake Pincus.

Jawbreaker's 24 Hour Revenge Therapy (33 1/3 series)

Ronen Givony

A 33 1/3 series entry on Jawbreaker's 1994 emo/punk landmark 24 Hour Revenge Therapy, examining the album's role in the punk-to-emo transition and the band's fraught major-label-adjacent position within the 1990s independent scene.

Johann Sebastian Bach's Art of Fugue: Performance Practice Based on German Eighteenth-Century Theory

Ewald Demeyere

Demeyere reconstructs how Bach's late, abstract keyboard-independent counterpoint cycle Die Kunst der Fuge should actually sound, grounding tempo, articulation, ornamentation and instrumentation choices in period German theoretical treatises rather than modern performance convention.

Johann Sebastian Bach's St John Passion (BWV 245): A Theological Commentary

Andreas Loewe

Andreas Loewe's Brill monograph reads Bach's St John Passion movement by movement as Lutheran theology set to music, tracing how the libretto's chorales, arias and turba choruses embody eighteenth-century Leipzig devotional and doctrinal concerns.

Kate Bush's Hounds of Love (33 1/3 series)

Leah Kardos

A 33 1/3 series study of Kate Bush's 1985 landmark Hounds of Love, examining its Fairlight-CMI-driven production, concept-album structure (the 'Ninth Wave' suite), and status as a high-water mark of art-pop studio craft.

Ludomusicology: Approaches to Video Game Music

Michiel Kamp, Tim Summers, Mark Sweeney, and Melanie Fritsch (eds.)

An Equinox Publishing edited collection that helped establish 'ludomusicology' as a named academic field, gathering essays applying diverse musicological, game-studies, and sound-design frameworks to video game music and audio.

Music and Esotericism

Laurence Wuidar (ed.)

Volume 9 of Brill's Aries Book Series (Texts and Studies in Western Esotericism), an edited scholarly collection on the historical intersections of music with occultism, magic, and esoteric philosophy, from an editorial board including Wouter Hanegraaff and Antoine Faivre.

Music and the Muses: The Culture of Mousike in the Classical Athenian City

Penelope Murray and Peter Wilson (eds.)

An Oxford University Press edited volume on mousike — the combined art of music, poetry and dance central to classical Athenian civic and cultural life. NOTE: the local PDF (49 pages) is only the book's bibliography, not the full text.

Piano Opera: Final Fantasy I II III (Sheet Music)

Nobuo Uematsu (composer); arr. Hiroyuki Nakayama

A Japanese piano-arrangement songbook of Nobuo Uematsu's scores for the original NES-era Final Fantasy I-III (composed for the Famicom's 2A03 sound chip), arranged for solo piano by Hiroyuki Nakayama, with composer/arranger staff profiles.

Popular Music in the Nostalgia Video Game: The Way It Never Sounded

Andra Ivănescu

A Palgrave 'Studies in Audio-Visual Culture' monograph examining how nostalgia-themed and retro-styled video games deploy popular music (and evoke chiptune/retro sound) to construct idealized, often historically inaccurate, memories of past eras and past games.

Synthesizer Basics

Dean Friedman

A classic 1980s Amsco beginner's guide (by singer-songwriter Dean Friedman) to understanding, programming and using a synthesizer, with dedicated sections on the Yamaha DX7, sampling devices, and MIDI.

The Jesus Lizard Book

Various (band members, Corey Rusk, and contributors)

An oral-history/coffee-table retrospective of noise-rock band the Jesus Lizard, told through the band members' and Touch and Go Records founder Corey Rusk's own words, moving album by album from Pure (1989) through Blue (1997).

The Organ Preludes of Johann Sebastian Bach

George B. Stauffer

Stauffer's 1980 musicological monograph (Studies in Musicology series, UMI Research Press) traces the stylistic development, sources, and compositional structure of Bach's free organ preludes across his career, from early North German influence to the mature Leipzig works.

The Recording Engineer's Handbook, Second Edition

Bobby Owsinski

Owsinski's widely used practical reference covers microphones, preamps, stereo and multichannel miking technique, DAW recording fundamentals (ADCs/DACs, latency, headroom), drum-kit and instrument miking, and mixing — the standard textbook for recording-engineering craft.

The Rock Synthesizer Manual: A Revised Guide for the Electronic Musician

Geary Yelton

A 1984 hardware-era synthesizer manual walking rock/pop musicians through electronic sound fundamentals — voltage control, VCAs, envelope generation, filtering, modulation, keyboard controllers, sequencers, sample-and-hold, and patch-memory programming.

The Routledge Research Companion to Johann Sebastian Bach

Robin A. Leaver (ed.)

An edited scholarly survey aimed at graduate-level Bach researchers, with specialist essays assessing the past 30-50 years of Bach research (chronology, sources, performance practice, authorship debates) and flagging open questions for future study.

The Second Sense: Studies in Hearing and Musical Judgement from Antiquity to the Seventeenth Century

Charles Burnett, Michael Fend, and Penelope Gouk (eds.)

A Warburg Institute Surveys and Texts volume (No. XXII) collecting essays on the history of theories of hearing and musical judgment from Aristotle through the seventeenth century, spanning philosophy of perception, music theory, and early acoustic/mechanical science.

They Might Be Giants' Flood (33 1/3 series)

Philip Sandifer and S. Alexander Reed

A 33 1/3 series study of They Might Be Giants' 1990 breakthrough Flood, co-written by Philip Sandifer and S. Alexander Reed, examining the album's genre-hopping songwriting, drum-machine-and-accordion aesthetic, and place in alternative-pop history.

Tools of the Trade: Digital Audio Workstation Usage Antecedents

Darko Etinger

A 13-page academic journal article (Informatologia, 2016), not a book: Etinger surveys 838 heavy metal/hard rock artists using Technology Acceptance and Task-Technology Fit models to identify what drives DAW adoption in composition, recording, mixing and mastering.

Tori Amos's Boys for Pele (33 1/3 series)

Amy Gentry

A 33 1/3 series study of Tori Amos's 1996 self-produced Boys for Pele, examining the album's confrontational piano-and-harpsichord-driven sound, self-production, and mythological/feminist conceptual framing.

Understanding Video Game Music

Tim Summers

A Cambridge University Press musicology textbook developing analytical methods and concepts for video game scores, using case studies like Final Fantasy VII, Monkey Island 2, SSX Tricky and Silent Hill to build a framework the author summarizes as 'music for playing.'

World Music: The Basics

Richard O. Nidel

A Routledge 'Basics' student reference surveying global popular and traditional musics region by region — Africa, the Caribbean, Latin America, Europe, Asia and the Middle East — as an introductory map of styles, instruments and key artists rather than a technical or theory text.
