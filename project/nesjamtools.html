NESjamtools

STABLE  C:\Dev\NESjamtools · Python (numpy/scipy render core), JSFX (live instrument), REAPER .rpp generation, MIDI

NESjamtools is the instrument layer of a three-project family (Archive = NSFRIPPER, Instrument = this project, Score = shared MIDI). It doesn't try to be a byte-exact emulator; nesjam/nes_render.py is a hand-tuned NES-hardware-flavored renderer (non-linear DAC mix, bandlimited pulse synthesis, LFSR noise, triangle gate-hold, 4-pole analog lowpass and DC blocking) that turns a MIDI score into an accurate WAV bounce, while instruments/NESJam.jsfx is a single multitimbral REAPER plugin instance playing all four 2A03 voices live (channel 1->Pulse1, 2->Pulse2, 3->Triangle, 4->Noise).

build_all.py batches 50 representative NES games (Castlevania, Mega Man 2/3/4, Zelda, Metroid, SMB, Contra and more), sourcing from NSFRIPPER's output. Four games (Contra, Battletoads, Zelda, Wizards & Warriors) additionally ship as 'hybrid' projects -- accurate audio stems playing by default with muted, editable NESJam MIDI tracks underneath -- and as pure 4-track all-synth variants. A second install-free-VST path via the free 'Magical 8bit Plug 2' is documented as an alternative, including per-game ADSR translation tables.

HOWTO_LIVE_KEYBOARD.md walks through a dedicated _KEYBOARD_TEST REAPER project to isolate keyboard/synth problems from song-data problems. output.md (2026-06-11) is a detailed post-mortem mapping every specific complaint from an ear-test session (W&W noise sustaining after stop, Zelda pops on note edges, Double Dragon II sounding like noise from a mis-picked SFX-bank track, wrong track colors, a MIDI-device slot bug) to root causes and fixes -- all logged as Fixed except one, explicitly marked 'Awaiting your ears.' CLAUDE.md states the hard rule directly: don't claim 'sounds like the game' until the user ear-confirms in REAPER; the WAV renders are the verifiable artifact, the JSFX is unverified until loaded.

Assets on disk: 84 MIDI files, 23 four-track MIDI files, 50 NSF files, 50 game folders under projects/ and renders/. Git history is a single commit with no remote configured -- never pushed anywhere.

NESjamtools' own CLAUDE.md states the three-project "Archive / Instrument / Score" framing directly, in close to these words: Archive -- byte-exact register replay -- "NOT our job, that's NSFRIPPER"; Instrument -- a score or keyboard that sounds like the game -- "THIS is the goal"; Score -- editable MIDI, reused as-is from NSFRIPPER's own extraction work. That's a real division of labor stated by the project itself, not an outside inference. A small asset-count discrepancy is worth noting plainly: the `projects/` directory currently holds 54 subfolders, two of which are test scaffolds (`_KEYBOARD_TEST`, `_MAGICAL_TEST`), leaving 52 real game folders against the README's stated "50 games" -- a minor, unresolved gap between the documented and actual count. `renders/` holds 52 game directories and 71 WAV files total.

The ear-test postmortem (`output.md`) tracks four rounds of listening and fixes, game by game, and is worth citing specifically because it's the clearest evidence in this whole family of what "verify by ear" actually looks like in practice rather than as a slogan. Wizards & Warriors' noise channel sustaining after the note should have stopped traced to two separate bugs -- CC11 volume automation ignoring note-off events, and no handling for CC123 (all-notes-off) at all -- and was confirmed fixed by measuring the noise stem's RMS level drop from 0.826 to effectively 0.000000 after note-off, not just by ear. Zelda's popping on note edges traced to a missing DC blocker and was fixed the same way NSFRIPPER's own rules require (a proper high-pass filter, not a subtracted constant). Kid Icarus's over-long sustains were the same CC11 bug hitting a particularly "dense automator" driver; its FDS expansion-chip lead voice remains an acknowledged, documented, unrendered gap. Double Dragon II's reported "wall of noise" traced to a filename parser reading a literal "2" inside the game's title as a track index, which happened to select a sound-effects loop over the real song on a tiebreak -- fixing the parser also silently corrected the wrong track selection in seven other games (Super Mario Bros. 3, Zelda II, Castlevania II and III, Mega Man 2, Chip 'n Dale 2, and Ninja Gaiden II), a good example of one bug fix clearing several unrelated-looking symptoms at once. A reported "muffled reverby thud" on the Wizards & Warriors title-screen patch, played live from a keyboard, was run down with a small standalone Python port of the synth's DSP rendering a held middle C -- confirming correct pitch (262 Hz), a bright rather than muffled spectral centroid (6,907 Hz), a steady sustain, and no reverb anywhere in the signal path -- and was ultimately attributed to a REAPER monitoring/routing artifact on the tester's machine rather than a synthesis bug, a useful example of a complaint that DSP inspection cleared rather than confirmed. The one item still open at time of writing, "W&W Map: rhythm feels wrong," is explicitly marked "Awaiting your ears" rather than closed with a guessed fix -- consistent with the project's own stated rule (in its CLAUDE.md) not to claim a track "sounds like the game" until Ted confirms it directly in REAPER.
