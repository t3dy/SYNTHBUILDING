GlitchMario

STABLE  C:\Dev\GlitchMario · Vanilla HTML/CSS/JS (site); Mesen 2, Python (mido, py65), REAPER + JSFX (pipeline it documents)

▶ live site

GlitchMario documents an NES-audio reverse-engineering effort as a narrative artifact rather than shipping a tool. The pipeline it describes: NES hardware APU -> Mesen 2 emulator trace capture (30,362 register changes across 21 parameters, 9,496 frames) -> mesen_to_midi.py (period-change detection, CC11/CC12 automation, producing 796 notes and 7,064 CC events) -> generate_project.py (a REAPER project with inline MIDI, 4 tracks) -> ReapNES_Console.jsfx (NES APU emulation inside REAPER: pulse, triangle, noise/LFSR, drum table) -> ear-verification, reaching '80% fidelity and climbing' by day five.

The site itself is a vanilla-JS single-page app: a 67.5KB data.json holds a project header plus an events array of 100 timeline entries (day/phase/title/summary/category/docs/terms), rendered by app.js into filterable timeline cards by category and by day, alongside a hardcoded 30-entry NES-history dataset (Famicom 1983 through GlitchMario 2026) and a 70+ term technical glossary.

Its greatest-hits blunder table is worth reading on its own: JSFX's '^' meaning exponentiation not XOR made the noise channel produce alien transmissions; a DC-offset formula that only worked at volume 15 caused drums to phase-invert mid-decay; a naive track-to-channel mapping turned Vampire Killer into a wall of sound; drums with no envelope sounded like popcorn; a synth ignoring CC11/CC12 silently replaced ground-truth envelopes with generic ADSR; a mixer formula error made pulse amplitude 15x too quiet while drums literally subtracted from the mix.

The site's own `data.json` (100 events) sorts every entry into one of seven categories -- architecture, blunder, discovery, glitch, milestone, narrative, rule -- which is what powers the timeline's category filter. Of the 17 blunders the project claims, 15 are individually identifiable as their own dated cards; the remaining two appear folded into adjacent narrative entries rather than standing alone, which is a minor structural quirk rather than a missing-content problem. Beyond the handful already well known from this cluster's other projects (the `^`-as-exponent noise bug, the volume-15-only DC-offset formula), the fuller list includes: a stray fourth Unicode/comment-parsing issue bundled into the same early blunder card as the `//tags:` and missing-pin mistakes; `SOURCE MIDIPOOL` items that render as visual-only stubs in the REAPER version used here, requiring `SOURCE MIDI` plus an explicit `FILE` reference instead; a gap in JSFX slider numbering that silently shifted parameter values on load; an early version of the project where every track played all four APU oscillators simultaneously before per-channel routing was added; a noise/drum channel that simply toggled on and off with no envelope until a 23-entry table mapping GM drum notes to period, mode, attack and decay values was built; a shipped Console synth that had no CC11/CC12 handlers at all, silently substituting generic ADSR envelopes for the ground-truth automation across all of its then-1,099 rendered projects; a minimal RPP header that loaded and played back fine but silently broke live keyboard input because it omitted several REAPER-required blocks (`ENVATTACH`, `SAMPLERATE`, `METRONOME`, `RECORD_CFG`, `MASTERPLAYSPEEDENV`, `TEMPOENVEX`, `PROJBAY`); and a `REC 0 0` (unarmed, no input selected) setting that later silently defaulted to the system microphone once a user armed the track.

The extraction numbers are more granular than the headline totals suggest: of the 30,362 captured register changes across 21 parameters, Pulse 1 contributes 353 notes, Pulse 2 341, Triangle 328 and Noise 168 in one internal count -- summing to 1,190, not the 796 figure quoted in the project's own README, a discrepancy this pass could not resolve and is flagging rather than silently picking one number. Other specifics: Pulse 1's driver caps its own volume at 12 rather than the hardware's full range of 15, and Pulse 2 caps at 11 -- a deliberate headroom choice in the original game's driver, not an extraction artifact. The triangle channel's linear counter takes 27 distinct values across 3,644 separate changes, meaning its "on/off" gating is considerably more nuanced than a simple gate. The noise channel uses seven distinct hardware period values, collapsed down to just four General MIDI drum categories in the final MIDI export -- the project's own docs call this lost distinction "the missing 30%" of noise-channel fidelity. The project's own closing fidelity scorecard rates itself unevenly by parameter rather than with one blanket number: pitch 98%, timing 94%, pulse volume 95%, pulse duty 100%, triangle gating 70%, noise envelope 30%, noise timbre 50%, with DPCM sample channel not applicable to this particular song -- a weighted overall of roughly 80%, matching the README's "80% fidelity and climbing" framing but showing plainly that the noise channel, not pitch or timing, is where the real remaining gap sits.
