NESMusicStudio

ACTIVE  C:\Dev\NESMusicStudio · Python, REAPER, JSFX, ffmpeg (WAV/MP4/YouTube pipeline)

▶ live site

NESMusicStudio shares NSFRIPPER's exact pipeline shape (NSF/ROM -> MIDI -> REAPER/WAV/MP4 -> YouTube) and may represent where that lineage actually continued under a different folder name after NSFRIPPER's last commit in April 2026 -- this needs a direct comparison pass before assuming NSFRIPPER itself is the most current state of the work. It is git-committed and shows genuine recent activity, unlike several of its siblings.

Confirmed progress: Castlevania 1 is complete with all 15 tracks extracted and validated against ground truth (0 pitch mismatches). Contra is in progress with all 11 tracks extracted and a 96.6% volume match against reference. The project deploys a live site at t3dy.github.io/ReapNES/.

A hardcoded path inside NSFRIPPER's own build_bach_preview.py (C:\Dev\NESMusicStudio\output\bach_mashups) references this exact folder name, confirming shared or overlapping lineage between the two projects -- worth resolving which one is now canonical before doing further extraction work in either.

`docs/GAME_MATRIX.md` gives real per-track fidelity numbers beyond the headline "complete"/"in progress" labels: Castlevania 1's 15 of 15 tracks measure 99.5% pulse-channel fidelity (zero pitch mismatches across 1,792 checked frames, with 9 pulse and 8 triangle "sounding" mismatches traced to fade-step edge cases and a one-frame linear-counter timing offset respectively) and 99.5% triangle fidelity. Contra's 11 of 11 tracks (validated against a 2,976-frame trace of the Jungle-theme) measure less evenly: 91% pulse-1 pitch match, 82% pulse-1 volume match, 70% triangle pitch match, and a notably higher 94% triangle volume match -- meaning Contra's remaining gap is concentrated specifically in triangle-channel pitch tracking, not a uniform shortfall across the whole extraction.

The project's own `docs/331UPDATE.md` (dated 2026-03-31) is explicit about lineage in a way that revises the open question `NSFRIPPER.md` raises: it states that "the NESMusicStudio (extraction pipeline) and ReapNES-Studio (synth/REAPER tooling) repos were brought together" -- a documented merge with ReapNES-Studio specifically, not with NSFRIPPER, and no document anywhere in this project claims NSFRIPPER lineage directly. As of that same update the project held 701 REAPER projects across 34 games, with 14 games added in that single session alone (Dragon Warrior, Final Fantasy, Metal Gear, both Ultima titles, Faxanadu, Gargoyle's Quest II, The Goonies II, Kid Icarus, Legendary Wings, Section Z, Silver Surfer, Strider, and Trojan) -- a considerably larger and more actively growing catalog than the two-game Castlevania/Contra headline suggests. Its docs also record specific per-game envelope "signatures" for five games' NES sound drivers, worth citing as evidence of real domain analysis rather than assumption: Super Mario Bros. uses a uniform five-step volume decay (64-56-48-40-32) with fixed seven-frame note lengths; Castlevania decays faster, in four steps (32-24-16-8); Mega Man 2 uses a minimal two-step decay (64-16) with one-frame arpeggios averaging 1.5 frames on Pulse 2; Metroid produces a distinctive "breathing" crescendo-decrescendo shape with long, averaging-38-frame notes; and Contra's driver produces a much larger attack transient, stepping through five volume values (127-51-42-34-25-17) at note onset.

The YouTube pipeline is a real, working ffmpeg invocation rather than a described intention: it pads a still screenshot to even pixel dimensions, encodes video with libx264 in still-image tuning mode at one frame per second, and encodes audio as AAC at 192kbps, falling back to a generated solid black frame via ffmpeg's `lavfi` color source when no screenshot is available for a given track -- the script also auto-generates a YouTube description file that names the specific sound driver identified for that game (for example, "Konami Pre-VRC, Maezawa variant" for one Castlevania-family title) alongside a timestamped track listing, mirroring the description-generation approach `build_bach_preview.py` uses for the Chipped On Bach previews.
