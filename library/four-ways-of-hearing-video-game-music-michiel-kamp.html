Four Ways of Hearing Video Game Music

Michiel Kamp · E:\pdf\music\Michiel Kamp Four Ways of Hearing Video Game Music Oxford University Press.pdf

Michiel Kamp's Four Ways of Hearing Video Game Music is an academic monograph within the ludomusicology field established by scholars like Karen Collins (also in this library) and the Ludomusicology research network Kamp co-edits (see the companion volume Ludomusicology, also in Ted's collection). The book's central project, as its title indicates, is to propose and defend a typology of four distinct modes or perspectives from which video game music can be meaningfully 'heard' — likely spanning something like structural/formal listening (the score as composed music), functional/gameplay listening (music as feedback and signal within play), immersive/diegetic listening (music as part of the game world), and reflective/critical listening (music as an object of aesthetic judgment or cultural commentary), building on and extending frameworks from film-music theory (which has long distinguished diegetic from non-diegetic and background from foreground listening) while addressing what makes game music's listening situation distinctively interactive rather than fixed. The book engages closely with specific games and their scores as case studies to substantiate each listening mode, situating itself within broader film-music and sound-studies theory as much as within game studies specifically. It is written for an academic audience in musicology, media studies, and game studies — it assumes familiarity with core music-theoretical and film-sound-theory vocabulary and is a work of theory-building, not a history or a how-to-compose guide. For SYNTHBUILDER, this book is more conceptually than technically useful: it offers no synthesis, DSP, or programming content, but its close attention to how listeners actually process game audio during interactive play is relevant if SYNTHBUILDER's chiptune work ever moves beyond static tracks toward interactive or game-responsive audio, giving vocabulary for thinking about what a chiptune voice needs to communicate functionally (not just faithfully reproduce NES timbre) within a play context.

Relevance to SYNTHBUILDER
Low-Medium — theoretical framework for how players perceive game music during play; useful conceptual grounding for interactive audio design, no technical/DSP content.
