'The Temple of Music' by Robert Fludd is Peter Hauge's scholarly critical edition of Robert Fludd's Templum Musicae (published as part of Fludd's larger Utriusque Cosmi... Historia, 1617-1618), issued by Routledge/Ashgate as part of the series 'Music Theory in Britain, 1500-1700: Critical Editions,' general-edited by Jessie Ann Owens (UC Davis). The series' stated mission (per its own front-matter description, captured directly) is 'the first systematic attempt to present the entire range of theoretical writing about music by English, Scottish, Welsh and Irish writers from 1500 to 1700 in modern critical editions,' using original spelling and situating each work in its historical and musical-practice context; companion volumes in the same series (also listed in the front matter) cover Thomas Campion and Giovanni Coprario on counterpoint and composition, William Bathe's introduction to singing, and John Birchensha's writings on music. Fludd — an English physician, alchemist, and Hermetic-Rosicrucian-adjacent cosmological philosopher already well represented elsewhere in Ted's renaissance-magic collection — presents music within his characteristic macrocosm/microcosm cosmological framework: for Fludd, musical harmony is not merely an aesthetic or practical craft but a direct expression of universal cosmic proportion, continuous with his broader engagement with number, geometry, and correspondence across creation. Hauge's edition provides the original text (likely with translation, given the series' English-language readership), extensive scholarly annotation situating Fludd's musical theory against both contemporary English music theory and his own idiosyncratic Hermetic philosophy, and discussion of how (or whether) Fludd's cosmological framework maps onto period musical practice. It is written for musicologists and historians of Renaissance esotericism/science — dense critical-edition scholarship, not an accessible introduction. For SYNTHBUILDER this has no direct technical relevance — no synthesis, DSP, or sound-technology content — but as an actual period music-theory treatise (rather than criticism about music), it is the closest thing in Ted's library to primary-source Renaissance harmonic theory, and connects meaningfully to the same Hermetic/cosmological-correspondence thinking already central to ALCHEMYBLOCKSHOOTER and Ted's broader esoteric-studies corpus, should a future project want to ground synth patch design in period harmonic-cosmological theory rather than pure NES-accuracy.

The "Music Theory in Britain, 1500-1700" series this critical edition belongs to describes its own mission directly in its front matter — quoted above — as the first systematic modern critical-edition project covering the full range of English, Scottish, Welsh and Irish music theory across two centuries, general-edited by Jessie Ann Owens; its companion volumes on Thomas Campion and Giovanni Coprario (counterpoint and composition), William Bathe (an introduction to singing), and John Birchensha situate Fludd's Templum Musicae within a specific, coherent editorial program rather than as an isolated curiosity. Fludd himself is already a significant figure elsewhere in Ted's collection and project work — an English physician, alchemist, and Hermetic-Rosicrucian-adjacent cosmological philosopher whose broader Utriusque Cosmi... Historia (of which the Templum Musicae is one part) is exactly the kind of primary source Ted's esotericism-focused digital-humanities portals (Claudiens on Michael Maier, the broader Rosicrucian materials) are built to catalog and contextualize. What makes this book distinct from Wuidar's Music and Esotericism (also in this library, and likely to discuss Fludd as one figure among many) is that Hauge's edition gives access to Fludd's actual musical-theoretical argument in his own terms, with scholarly annotation, rather than a modern scholar's synthesis about the broader tradition Fludd belongs to — it is primary source plus apparatus, not secondary survey. For SYNTHBUILDER, as with the Wuidar volume, the honest relevance is thematic rather than technical: no synthesis, DSP, or sound-engineering content, but a genuine, citable primary-source anchor if a future project wanted to design a synth patch or a sound-based mechanic explicitly around Fludd's specific macrocosm/microcosm harmonic correspondences rather than a generalized "esoteric" gesture.

This summary is based on the edition's confirmed series context, editorial framing, and Fludd's well-documented broader work, not a close reading of Hauge's specific translation and commentary.
