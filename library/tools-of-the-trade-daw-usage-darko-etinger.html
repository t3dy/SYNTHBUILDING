Tools of the Trade: Digital Audio Workstation Usage Antecedents

Darko Etinger · E:\pdf\music\Bach\[Informatologia 2016-jul 1 vol. 49 iss. 1-2] Tools of the trade_ Digital audio workstation usage antecedents{Etinger, Darko (author)}(2016, Croatian Communicologysts Association){109650636} libgen.li.pdf

This item is a peer-reviewed journal article rather than a monograph, included in Ted's library because it was filed alongside the Bach materials (likely a keyword/DAW-related mis-sort). Darko Etinger, of Juraj Dobrila University of Pula, Croatia, investigates why individual musicians adopt digital audio workstations for composing, pre-production, recording, mixing, and mastering. The study's theoretical scaffolding combines the Task-Technology Fit (TTF) model with the Technology Acceptance Model (TAM), which together model the causal chain from perceived usefulness and perceived ease of use through to actual usage behavior — a standard information-systems research framework, applied here to music production tools rather than business software. The empirical base is a survey of 838 completed responses from heavy metal and hard rock artists specifically (chosen, per the abstract, because that genre community has been an early and heavy adopter of home-studio DAW workflows as an alternative to commercial studios), analyzed with partial least squares structural equation modeling (PLS-SEM). The article's framing draws on prior work (cited: Persson) arguing that affordable DAWs and home recording equipment created real competition for commercial studios and forced a re-evaluation of what gets recorded at home versus in a professional facility, and by whom. The piece is written for an audience of information-systems and media-technology researchers, not musicians or producers — it does not teach DAW use, mixing, or recording technique, and contains no discussion of specific DAW software features. For SYNTHBUILDER this article is essentially irrelevant: it is organizational/adoption-behavior research about why musicians choose DAWs, not a technical, DSP, or plugin-development resource, and it says nothing about REAPER, JSFX, VST3, or synthesis. It should be flagged on the showcase page, if included at all, as a minor curiosity in Ted's library rather than a substantive title — it may be worth omitting from a public-facing books shelf entirely, since it reads as filler rather than a book people would recognize.
