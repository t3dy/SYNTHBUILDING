Hole's Live Through This (33 1/3 series)

Anwen Crawford · E:\pdf\music\(33 1_3 (103), 3313) Anwen Crawford - Hole's Live Through This. 103-Bloomsbury Academic (2014).pdf

Anwen Crawford's entry in the 33 1/3 series covers Hole's second album, Live Through This (1994), released days after Kurt Cobain's death and permanently shadowed by controversy, grief, and gendered skepticism about frontwoman Courtney Love's authorship — the extracted opening pages (a dedication 'for L and for E — or, more especially, for the girls they once were' followed by an epigraph from Euripides's Medea, 'My reputation, yet again! It goes before me like a curse') signal a critical approach centered on reputation, gender, and the way Love's story has overshadowed close engagement with the record itself. As with other 33 1/3 volumes, expect a blend of close listening/track analysis, band and scene history (Hole's place within Riot Grrrl-adjacent and mainstream grunge-era alternative rock), and cultural criticism addressing the persistent doubt cast on Love's songwriting credit and the album's reception in the immediate aftermath of Cobain's death. The book is written for readers invested in 1990s alternative-rock and feminist music history and criticism; it assumes no technical music background and offers no production or synthesis instruction, functioning as long-form music criticism and cultural history rather than a how-to text. For SYNTHBUILDER this book has no technical relevance whatsoever — no synthesis, DSP, recording technique, or sound design content — and its subject (raw, guitar-driven 1990s alternative rock) is stylistically unrelated to chiptune or synthesizer programming. Its place in Ted's library and on a music-showcase page is purely as evidence of broad, personally significant listening and critical interest, alongside the other 33 1/3 titles in the collection, not as a resource this project would draw on for building synths or plugins.
