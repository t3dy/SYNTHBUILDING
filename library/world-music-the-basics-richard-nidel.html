World Music: The Basics

Richard O. Nidel · E:\pdf\music\Bach\[Routledge Student Reference. The Basics] Richard O. Nidel - World Music (2010, Routledge) - libgen.li.pdf

World Music: The Basics belongs to Routledge's 'The Basics' student-reference series, and is structured accordingly: a broad, accessible survey rather than a specialist monograph. At 880 extracted pages of text (a large, dense reference), Nidel organizes the book geographically, moving through major world-music traditions and their popular-music offshoots — the front matter and structure follow the standard 'world music' curriculum used in introductory ethnomusicology and popular-music-studies courses: African popular musics (highlife, Afrobeat, soukous and related genres), Caribbean and Latin American traditions (reggae, salsa, samba, and their diasporic developments), music of the Middle East and North Africa, South and East Asian traditions, and European folk and roots musics, generally covering instrumentation, historical development, key figures, and the traditions' relationship to Western popular music and the recording industry. As a 'Basics' title it is written for undergraduates and general readers encountering these traditions for the first time — it assumes no prior musicological training, favors accessible prose and representative artist/recording references over deep theoretical or ethnomusicological analysis, and is explicitly a survey/overview text rather than an argument-driven scholarly monograph. For SYNTHBUILDER this book offers essentially nothing technical — no synthesis, DSP, DAW, or chiptune content — and its relevance to REAPER/JSFX plugin building is nil. Its value to Ted is as general musical-breadth background: exposure to scales, rhythmic structures, and instrument timbres outside the Western equal-tempered, four-square-time-signature tradition that dominates most synthesis literature, which could inform more adventurous chiptune composition (non-Western modes or rhythms rendered in 8-bit voices) but is not itself a how-to resource for building synths or plugins. On the showcase page it belongs in a 'music history and breadth' category rather than the technical/production shelf.
