Karpathy’s new `autoresearch` repo is a **minimal autonomous experiment loop for ML research**. The setup is deliberately narrow: an AI coding agent edits only `train.py`, runs a fixed 5-minute training job, checks whether validation bits-per-byte (`val_bpb`) improved, keeps or discards the change, and repeats. The repo is built around a simplified single-GPU `nanochat` training stack, with `prepare.py` kept fixed, `train.py` mutable, and `program.md` serving as the human-written research brief for the agent. He describes the broader direction as moving toward massively collaborative asynchronous agents, “SETI@home style.” citeturn1view0turn0search0

The important idea is not “AI does science in general.” It is much narrower and more useful: **you freeze the environment, expose one editable surface, define one score, and let the agent grind the loop**. In Karpathy’s repo, the frozen parts are the data pipeline and constants; the editable surface is `train.py`; the score is `val_bpb`; and the fixed 5-minute wall-clock budget makes experiments comparable on one machine. The repo says this yields about 12 experiments per hour and roughly 100 overnight. citeturn1view0

There is also now a separate March 2026 arXiv preprint, `AutoResearch-RL`, that formalizes this pattern as an RL problem: state = current code plus experiment history, action = a structured code diff, reward = validation performance. That paper is not Karpathy’s repo itself; it is an academic extension inspired by his prototype. citeturn1view1
